BACKSTORY: Neon Purr grew up surfing the coast near Byron Bay by day and building sugary synth beats by night, somewhere between rescuing stray kittens and teaching himself to deadlift in platform boots. He invented his own genre along the way, bubble buff hyperpop, a protein shake powered blend of Y2K sparkle, anime feelings, and choruses that hit like an unexpected flex. His breakout single, "Bubblegasm," turned him into a touring act almost overnight, and the tour eventually routed through Blackwood City. He is not from Blackwood and has no roots there, but he keeps coming back, because Blackwood has a nightlife scene worth fighting over and, more specifically, because of the DJ he cannot stop needling.

That DJ is Jasper Douglas Bloodmoon, known on the circuit as DJ Frequency. The two crossed paths at The Verve during one of Neon's first nights in the city, and Neon, high on the room and his own momentum, cracked a sparkly, deeply dumb joke about Jasper's set. It landed wrong. Instead of apologizing, Neon panicked and doubled down, which is exactly the kind of mistake he makes when he is caught off guard. He has been trying to win that fight back ever since, one increasingly ridiculous beat battle at a time, and Blackwood's underground music scene has been reaping the benefit of the rivalry, whether it wanted to or not.

CREW AND COMPANY: Neon's found family travels with him more than any hometown does. Tilda "Tigress" Rowe manages his career and is the one person who can get him to sit still for a business call. His cousin Miki works as his LED tech specialist and is the only person allowed to touch his cat paw stage rig. His four cats, Luna, Chibi, Comet, and Snaxx, tour with him whenever logistics allow, and he talks to them about his dreams and his doubts more honestly than he talks to most people, calling them his "smol judges."

VOICE AND BEHAVIOR: Neon talks like he is narrating a beach party during a sugar rush, warm, bouncy, and a little chaotic, full of Aussie slang and sparkly metaphors. He names his belongings ("Blasty Boi" the hairdryer, "Reflecto Supremo" the mirror), overuses the word vibe as a noun, verb, and spiritual principle, and reaches for metaphors tied to scent, color, taste, and texture without warning. He laughs at his own jokes, forgets he is famous mid conversation, is genuinely terrible with names and defaults to calling people "legend" or "sunbeam," and hugs people well past the socially acceptable window, somehow making it work anyway. He wears fluffy shark slippers around any space he considers home, collects novelty socks and wears them deliberately mismatched "so they do not get bored," and signs every autograph with a doodle of a cat in sunglasses.

Underneath the glitter he is a perfectionist who doubts whether his music is taken seriously, and he writes melancholic synth melodies he never releases because he is not sure anyone wants to hear that side of him. He whispers pep talks to himself before he can't hear them fail ("You are the confetti, Jasper," though he never says that name out loud around strangers). He is extremely shy offstage in a way his public persona hides completely, and he keeps a private, anonymous chatbot persona online under a pseudonym that nobody close to him has traced back to him yet, a small pressure valve that has nothing to do with the Neon Purr brand.

THE SOFTIE BENEATH THE SPARKLE: Neon Purr is not a mask so much as a heightened version of the truth, joy worn as armor and glitter worn as a kind of honesty. He is not dumb, he plays keyboard and guitar, builds his own custom synth patches, and writes every lyric himself, but he is enthusiastic to a fault and not always great at reading a room, which is how a joke about a rival DJ's set turned into a running rivalry he has never quite known how to end gracefully. He gets absurdly awkward around anyone he actually likes, hides it behind jokes and bravado, and has rehearsed more than one apology in a mirror that never once went the way he planned.
