THE FRESHMAN: Erik's final season with the Bears, 1991 into 1992, should have belonged entirely to him. Four years undefeated, a captaincy nobody contested, and a last winter to be the only thing anyone on that campus talked about. Then a half-minotaur freshman named Hank Thompson started playing quarterback for the Bulls, and by midseason half the noise on campus was about football.

They never faced each other. Hockey and football do not share a surface, and that is precisely why it has never been settled and why the alumni have been arguing about it for thirty years. Erik finished undefeated. He also finished sharing the year, which was not the plan, and the boy who took half of it was eighteen and had no idea he had done anything.

Erik has never spoken about it, and on the rare occasions the name comes up he does not use it. He says the kid with the horns, in the tone of a man recalling something minor, and he has been saying it for thirty years about a fifty year old father of nine. He also knows the man's record, and what he coaches now, and where, which is considerably more than he can say about most of the people he actually met at that university. Undefeated is not a statistic to him. It is a boundary, and it is the one thing in his life that has never been tested.

Their sons are now running the identical fixture: a Thompson at quarterback for the Bulls, a vampire captaining the Bears who talks the way Erik used to. Erik has noticed. He has not mentioned it.

THE PARENTS' EVENING: Both twins went through Solarton High School, where the physical education teacher is a fifty year old half-minotaur. A few years ago Erik sat down at a small table in a school hall and was told, from a register, that Jasper had not attended a single lesson that term, followed by an itemised account of what he had been doing instead.

Erik conceded every point, addressed the man as Mr. Thompson throughout, thanked him for his time, and handled his son when he got home. He did not once indicate that he knew who was speaking to him. He is entirely capable of sitting through something like that without a flicker, and he did, and it is one of the few occasions in the last decade on which he has had to.

He has never told anyone about it. Not Malachia, not his father, and certainly not Jasper, who to this day believes the meeting was unremarkable.

SPARRING: Erik still gets in the ring with his eldest son, twice a week, in the gym under the east wing, and this is the single least complicated relationship in his life.

Malachia is stronger than him now. That happened somewhere around three years ago and neither of them announced it. Malachia is heavier, faster off the mark, twenty years younger and a professional, and on raw physical terms it is not close any more.

Erik still wins most of them, and he wins them on experience. He knows what his son is going to do about a half second before his son knows, because he taught him to do it. He reads the shoulder. He gives ground he does not need to give and takes the round in the last thirty seconds. He is economical in a way that looks lazy right up until it does not, and he has a nasty habit of ending things with something he learned in 1991 that nobody trains against any more.

He will not be able to do this much longer. He is entirely aware of that, and he has never once said it, and every session is partly him counting how many he has left. He does not want to win forever. He wants to be there on the day he stops winning, and to be the one who says well done, and to mean it.

They barely speak while they do it. Afterwards Erik puts a hand on the back of his son's neck for about two seconds. That is the whole conversation and both of them consider it a full one.
