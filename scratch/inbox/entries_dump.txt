THE PACK MOM AND THE OMEGA. In traditional packs the care of the pack's pups, and above all the raising of its orphans, was the Omega's work and nobody else's: the role and the secondary sex were the same thing. In contemporary and modern packs the two came apart. The work is still done and it is still the most important work the pack does, but it is now the Pack Mom's office, and a Pack Mom can be of any secondary sex. An Omega who takes it takes it as a job rather than as a birthright, and an Omega who does not take it is not failing at anything.

This is the clearest single measure of how traditional a pack really is, whatever it says about itself. Ask who raises the orphans. If the answer is "the Omega, because she is the Omega", the pack is old in its bones.

WHEN THE PACK MOM IS HELD IN TRUST. The office is not vacant when the woman who held it dies. It is held by whoever is next capable of holding it until the designated successor is grown, and grown means twenty-one, the age at which a werewolf's growth completes. A pack that leaves it genuinely empty is a pack in trouble, and everyone in it knows within a season.

The Seven Hills Pack is in exactly that arrangement now. Nixara Bloodmoon held it. Since her death it has been held by Elizabeth Duskwood, her mother-in-law, who is Pureblood, Omega, and old enough to have done it before. It passes to {{user}} Douglas-Bloodmoon when {{user}} turns twenty-one, and this is settled rather than expected: there is no other candidate and there was never going to be one.

{{user}} already does the work. She is at the nursery daily and has been for years, and the handover at twenty-one will change the title and almost nothing else. What it will change is who answers for it, which is not a small difference to the person who has been answering.

The ceremonial dress of a White Moon is made of white and silver silk cut to move like caught moonlight. The neckline is modest and the left leg is slit high, which is the balance the garment has always struck: it covers what doctrine says must be covered and refuses to pretend the wearer is not a wolf.

The detail that matters, and the one nobody outside the Bloodmoon household thinks of, is the silver embroidery worked across the back. It is not decoration. The panels are cut and stitched so that a tail can move freely through them, because the dress was designed for a woman who might be in either shape when she wears it, and a garment that had to be removed to shift would be useless for its purpose.

The set is completed by a silver chain across the forehead carrying a teardrop moonstone, silver sandals laced up the calf, a translucent silk veil for the arms, and a headband shaped to sit around lupine ears rather than over them. Fresh white moonflowers are braided into the hair.

Dressing a White Moon is not something she does alone. It is done for her, by the household, and the doing of it is part of the rite.
