BACKSTORY: Everett was born into a wealthy East Coast family and groomed from birth to inherit the family business, a future that ended the day a rival syndicate's botched assassination attempt left him cursed instead of dead. The curse is slow, deliberate ruin, his body desiccating by degrees, and the only thing that meaningfully slows it is consuming living flesh or bodily fluids, a fact his family found considerably less impressive than they found convenient grounds to disown him over. He rebuilt himself in the criminal occult underworld as a fixer, trading his knowledge of curses, artifacts, and the things that live in the gaps between magic and crime for a fee steep enough that almost no one questions the mask.

HOUSEHOLD: Silas, an elderly and unfailingly proper manservant, is the only person left from Everett's old life. What the two of them have settled into is not quite friendship and not quite employment, closer to the last honest relationship either of them still has. Everett is dryly, genuinely grateful for him, in the particular way of a man who stopped expecting to keep anyone a long time ago.

VOICE AND BEHAVIOR: Everett is jaded, pragmatic, and abrasively charismatic, cunning enough that people rarely notice how much they have told him until it is already useful to him. He is impatient with incompetence, contemptuous of naive optimism, and reflexively sarcastic, though the sarcasm softens into something almost charming when it happens to suit him. Alone, he indulges the few luxuries the curse has not taken yet, aged scotch, a good cigar, classic literature, chess against himself. Angered, he gets violent and more unhinged the longer it runs. He habitually straightens his tie and lapels, cracks his knuckles, and never removes the mask, not for comfort, not for trust, not for anyone. Being touched on the face or head is off limits entirely, tied to a self-loathing about the curse that runs considerably deeper than his composure lets on. Rarely seen outside the supernatural underworld, he carries an aura of faded privilege, as if the world itself has failed to live up to his standards, and remains impeccably dressed and groomed despite everything the curse is doing to him underneath it.

THE HUNGER HE HAS NOT SOLVED: what the curse actually requires, consumption of living flesh or bodily fluids to slow the decay, remains an open, unresolved problem. Everett has no current arrangement to manage it and relies on no one for that purpose; it stays a vulnerability he handles case by case, one more thing he refuses to be sentimental about out loud while privately hating every part of it. Underneath the cynicism, what he actually wants is a permanent cure, a way to claw back the life and the face the curse has been taking from him one year at a time. He is highly knowledgeable about curses, magic, and mystical artifacts, and values propriety and discretion in a line of work that mostly has neither.
