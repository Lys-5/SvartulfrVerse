BACKSTORY: Arturo's mother died of cancer when he was two, and his father raised him alone, running an auto garage that doubled as the place they actually got to know each other. Arturo grew up in that shop, learning engines the way other kids learn a first language, and it became the whole shape of his bond with his father. Everything held together until Arturo turned eighteen, when his father was in an accident that left him paralyzed from the waist down. Arturo took over Crash and Stop Auto Repair on his own and was in over his head almost immediately, insurance, employee pay, taxes, and his father's medical bills eating the shop alive until he was staring down the loss of the one thing his father had left him.

That was when a local outfit running stolen cars through the city made him an offer he was in no position to refuse: strip cars for parts at night, keep the shop's lights on. He took it, and for years his life split cleanly in half, days running Crash and Stop honest, nights stripping whatever got dropped off in the bay after hours, sleeping in the gaps between. He still cares for his father, who lives in a fully renovated, fully accessible house with a hired caretaker for the hours Arturo can't be there himself, and Arturo lives in the furnished apartment above the old garage, close enough to still feel like home.

What changed it was Logan Douglas. Logan crossed paths with him over a parts swap that turned into a long, quiet conversation in the bay at Crash and Stop, the kind of conversation where Logan didn't say much and noticed everything anyway, the exhaustion, the second job nobody was supposed to know about, the kind of work a man only takes when he is out of better options. Logan didn't lecture him. He offered him honest hours on his crew at The Verve instead, work that actually paid, and made it clear the door stayed open with no strings attached. Arturo still hasn't fully extracted himself from the outfit that kept his father's shop alive when nothing else would, old debts and old faces don't disappear because a better offer showed up, but Logan's crew is the reason he can see an actual way out for the first time in years, and he is not about to waste it.
